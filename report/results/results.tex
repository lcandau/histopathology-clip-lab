\subsection{Retrieval contrast}
\begin{itemize}
    \item I$\to$T R@1 = 0.6980 (matches accuracy).
    \item T$\to$I P@K \emph{worse} than the baseline (P@1: 0.40 vs 0.80; P@50: 0.53 vs 0.84).
    \item Reading: the baseline was trained on LC25000 $\to$ embedding space tuned for in-distribution ranking. PLIP zero-shot $\to$ wins classification, loses fine ranking.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pathology pretraining $\approx$ +7.3 macro-F1 on frozen CLIP transfer.
    \item Gain in benign-vs-malignant, not in subtype discrimination.
    \item Image clusters in the shared UMAP separate visibly better than the baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:plip-umap}), but the prompt markers still collapse --- domain pretraining helps the image side, not the (still-degenerate) text side.
    \item Feature adaptation confirmed as the bigger lever in \S\ref{sec:results-adaptation}; PLIP's frozen-domain gain is one-third of unfreezing's frozen-vs-adapted gain.
\end{itemize}

\section{Plain image classifier on the same features}
\label{sec:results-linear-probe}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Drops the CLIP architecture: no text encoder, no projection heads, no learnable temperature, no contrastive loss.
    \item Replaces with: ResNet50 $\to$ GAP $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ Dense(5) trained with sparse cross-entropy.
    \item Run at both feature-adaptation budgets (frozen and unfreeze30) so the comparison vs the CLIP variants in \S\ref{sec:results-baseline} and \S\ref{sec:results-adaptation} is a complete $2 \times 2$.
    \item Isolates what the CLIP architecture itself contributes once the image encoder, data and adaptation budget are held constant.
\end{itemize}

\begin{table}[ht]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
                                & \multicolumn{2}{c|}{\textbf{Frozen}}     & \multicolumn{2}{c|}{\textbf{Unfreeze last 30}} \\ \hline
    \textbf{Architecture}       & Acc.    & Macro F1 & Acc.            & Macro F1 \\ \hline
    CLIP (InfoNCE)              & 0.6356  & 0.6273   & \textbf{0.8984} & \textbf{0.8981} \\ \hline
    Plain (CE softmax)          & 0.6440  & 0.6369   & 0.8728          & 0.8723   \\ \hline
    $\Delta$ (CLIP -- plain CE) & $-0.0084$ & $-0.0096$ & $+0.0256$       & $+0.0258$  \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{CLIP vs plain CE classifier on the same ResNet50 backbone at each feature-adaptation budget. Frozen regime: tied within noise. Unfreeze30 regime: CLIP wins $+2.6$ macro-F1.}
    \label{tab:linear-probe}
\end{table}

\subsection{Interpretation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Frozen regime: CLIP and plain CE tie within noise. Both saturate at the ImageNet-features ceiling of $\sim 0.63$; the architecture choice is invisible when the feature space is fixed.
    \item Unfreeze30 regime: CLIP outperforms plain CE by $+2.6$ macro-F1, broad-based across every class (benign lung $+0.018$; lung\_aca $+0.022$; lung\_scc $+0.020$; benign colon $+0.038$; colon\_aca $+0.031$).
    \item Plausible mechanism: InfoNCE with five shared class-prompt targets acts as implicit regularisation against per-image memorisation. With $\sim 14$\,M trainable parameters in the unfrozen tail, requiring every sample of a class to align with the same prompt limits how much sample-specific information the model can store. The benefit only materialises once the model has the capacity to overfit, which is why the frozen regime sees no difference.
    \item The CLIP paradigm's value on a closed-set 5-class task is not raw accuracy. The $+2.6$ macro-F1 bonus under feature adaptation is real but secondary --- the paradigm's load-bearing contribution is capabilities a plain classifier cannot provide: open-vocabulary classification at inference, image--text retrieval, prompt-tuning as a controlled lever.
\end{itemize}

\section{Feature-level adaptation: partial unfreezing}
\label{sec:results-adaptation}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Same arch/loss/prompts/data/split as the baseline; only the trainable mask on ResNet50 changes.
    \item Last 30 of 178 ResNet50 layers unfrozen ($\approx 14.4$\,M extra trainable params on top of the projection heads).
    \item BatchNorm in the unfrozen tail updates its running stats during training (training mode) and uses them at eval.
    \item LR dropped 10$\times$ to $10^{-4}$ to protect the pretrained features.
\end{itemize}

\begin{table}[ht]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
    \hline
    \textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Baseline} & \textbf{Unfreeze last 30} \\ \hline
    Accuracy           & 0.6356 & \textbf{0.8984} \\ \hline
    Balanced accuracy  & 0.6356 & \textbf{0.8984} \\ \hline
    Macro F1           & 0.6273 & \textbf{0.8981} \\ \hline
    Weighted F1        & 0.6273 & \textbf{0.8981} \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Partial fine-tuning vs frozen baseline: global classification metrics on the held-out test set.}
    \label{tab:unfreeze30-global}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
    \item $+27.1$ macro-F1 over the baseline ($\approx 3.7\times$ the PLIP zero-shot gain).
    \item Broad-based across classes: every class $F_1 \geq 0.85$.
    \item Lung\_aca: $F_1\ 0.48 \to 0.85$ (worst baseline class; still hardest but no longer a failure mode).
    \item $+0.25$ macro-F1 over the frozen-features ceiling measured by the plain CE classifier ($\sim 0.64$, see~\S\ref{sec:results-linear-probe}). Headroom to fully-fine-tuned literature results on LC25000 ($\sim 0.95\text{--}0.99$, end-to-end training, often with stronger backbones) is harder to pin down precisely without running that regime ourselves.
\end{itemize}
